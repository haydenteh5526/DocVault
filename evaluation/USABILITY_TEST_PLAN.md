# Usability study plan

**Updated:** 2026-09-14
**Participants:** Five consenting adults minimum
**Results status:** Not yet collected

## Ethics and data handling

- Explain the project, tasks, recording method, voluntary participation and
  right to stop before beginning.
- Obtain recorded consent using the institution's approved process.
- Use a prepared non-sensitive document set and disposable accounts.
- Identify participants only as `P01`–`P05`; keep consent forms and raw notes
  outside the public repository.
- Do not collect passwords, personal documents or unnecessary demographics.

## Standard session

Use the same build, device category and task wording for every participant.
Avoid coaching unless the participant is blocked; record every intervention.

1. Register and verify an account.
2. Upload or photograph a provided document.
3. Confirm/correct extracted information.
4. Find one stated fact using search.
5. Ask the same fact as a natural-language question and inspect its source.
6. Find or set a warranty date.
7. Export or delete the provided test data.

Record task completion, time on task, wrong turns, errors, assistance and short
observational notes. Ask the participant to think aloud without leading them.

## Post-test measures

Administer the standard ten-item System Usability Scale using its normal
five-point response scale without rewriting the questions. Then ask:

- What was easiest?
- What was most confusing?
- Did you trust the OCR and AI answer? Why?
- What single change would improve the product most?

For SUS, subtract 1 from odd-item responses; subtract even-item responses from
5; sum and multiply by 2.5. Report individual scores, mean, range and the small
sample limitation. SUS is a perceived-usability measure, not a percentage.

## Exit and analysis

Delete disposable accounts, confirm no participant documents remain, and store
notes securely. Group observations into themes only after all sessions. Turn
repeated, high-impact issues into tracked changes and rerun the affected task
with at least one participant where feasible.
